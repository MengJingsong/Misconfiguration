# R23 — stage 3-a JIRA check (2026-11-24)

**Case:** [`commitlog_max_outstanding_replay_bytes--archive-restore-slow-disk-replay`](../cases/commitlog_max_outstanding_replay_bytes--archive-restore-slow-disk-replay/commitlog_max_outstanding_replay_bytes--archive-restore-slow-disk-replay.md)
(true positive, `verified-OOM · deployment` proposed, pending the researcher's review of the archive restore and the 100 ms
dm-delay data volume; retry of B8; 1 GiB heap OOM 3/3, largest failing 1,280 MiB).

**Mechanism checked (§4).** A documented point-in-time restore from the commit-log archive (snapshot sstables put back,
`restore_command` / `restore_directories` / `restore_point_in_time` set) replays every archived segment written after the
snapshot (≈ 8.8 memtable pools here); nothing ties that volume to the replaying heap, and `pointInTimeExceeded` is checked
in the apply task (`CommitLogReplayer.java:296`), so post-PIT mutations are still deserialized and queued. With the data
volume slow, all 32 appliers park on the full 256 MiB memtable pool while `main` keeps deserializing; the replay queue's
bound in `CommitLogReplayer.handleMutation` (1,024 mutations / 64 MiB, `CommitLogReplayer.java:516-528`) counts
**serialized** bytes, so 700–731 queued P64 mutations (K ≈ 44–46 MiB, under both limits) hold ≈ 620–640 MiB (14×) →
`OutOfMemoryError: Java heap space` during start-up replay. Holder = the replay queue of deserialized mutations (as B8 and
B15); trigger = replay of dense tiny-cell mutations at start-up, here sourced from a real archive restore (B8 emulated
the restore by deleting sstables).

**Label: `known-fixed (incomplete)`** (exact: CASSANDRA-8639; its fix in 5.0.9 bounds serialized bytes, not heap; no
issue reports the archive/point-in-time restore volume or a replay OOM during a restore)

## Reason

The under-count that turns R23's restore replay into a heap OOM is the CASSANDRA-8639 mechanism: replay of dense mutations
at start-up overwhelms the node because the in-flight bound does not reflect heap. Its fix is in 5.0.9
(`git merge-base --is-ancestor 1c41a9ac2c cassandra-5.0.9` → true) but counts serialized bytes instead of the
`unsharedHeapSize()` the issue proposed, so it does not cover the mechanism → `known-fixed (incomplete)` under the owner's
2026-11-23 ruling, as for B8 and B15; not a regression, and the root cause is not wrong. 8639 is rated **exact** (same
holder and trigger), consistent with B8, whose restore emulation R23 replaces by the shipped archive restore; B15 was
partial because its replay volume came from a heap change, which R23 drops. The restore-specific elements (an archive span
of many pools replayed on the same heap, PIT filtering after deserialization, a slow data volume during restore) are not
reported by any issue; stage 1's `new` for the restore shape is superseded under the 3-a rules, where an exact issue with
an incomplete fix in the pinned version decides the label. No newer fix exists on `cassandra-5.0` or trunk.
