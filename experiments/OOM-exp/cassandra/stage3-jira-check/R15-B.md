# R15-B — stage 3-a JIRA check (2026-11-24)

**Case:** [`ErrorCollector_errorMsgs--humongous-request-during-gc-thrash`](../cases/ErrorCollector_errorMsgs--humongous-request-during-gc-thrash/ErrorCollector_errorMsgs--humongous-request-during-gc-thrash.md),

not checked here).

**Mechanism checked (§4.1, condition B).** One PREPARE of `SELECT v FROM … WHERE k IN (?,…,?)` with 8,388,585 markers fits the
16 MiB frame and the per-IP in-flight limit; the ANTLR parser buffers ≈ 16.8 M `CommonToken`s (`BufferedTokenStream.tokens`), the
`bindVariables` / IN-value lists and ≈ 8.4 M `AbstractMarker$Raw` before the 65,535-marker check, which runs only after
`parseAndPrepare` (`QueryProcessor.java:758-759`). Three such requests OOM a 2 GiB heap in the parse (token/marker classes 86 % of
the dump, U/K ≈ 43); applying the same bound during the parse removes it.

**Label: `new`** (no exact or partial issue; closest related: CASSANDRA-5598, the after-parse check's origin).

## Reason

Stage 1 found nothing on the parse-time token/marker buffer (`new`). The re-run (`Too many markers`, `CommonToken` /
`BufferedTokenStream` / `CommonTokenStream`, bind markers or variables with memory/OOM, parser/ANTLR with OOM, large query strings,
IN-clause PREPARE with OOM, `65535` with markers) again finds only the after-parse check's origin (5598), a 6.0 refactor that keeps
the check after the parse (18813, verified on trunk), and single-request OOMs from bound **values** (12618/12769). No issue reports
parse-time memory of a statement with millions of markers, so no exact or partial match, no fix, no doc → `new`.
