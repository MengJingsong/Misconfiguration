# C10 — stage 3-b client trigger check (2026-11-23)

**Case file:** [`../cases/max_concurrent_range_requests--empty-first-batch-full-page-fanout/max_concurrent_range_requests--empty-first-batch-full-page-fanout.md`](../cases/max_concurrent_range_requests--empty-first-batch-full-page-fanout/max_concurrent_range_requests--empty-first-batch-full-page-fanout.md)

Analysis only (no node run). Code references are to the pinned 5.0.9 tree `systems/cassandra/source/`, paths below
`src/java/org/apache/cassandra/` unless a file under `conf/` is named.

**Mechanism.** A paged `SELECT … WHERE b = 1 ALLOW FILTERING` whose first merged sub-range has no match makes the
coordinator jump to `min(remaining sub-ranges, 10 × cores)` concurrent sub-range requests, each with the full 5,000-row
page limit, and hold every response until the page is assembled (≈ 0.93 GiB of heap regions per query, uncharged). Three
such queries at once exhaust a 2 GiB coordinator heap.

**Condition assessed:** T-2G (3 instances, RF 1 keyspace, coordinator heap 2 GiB, Q = 3 concurrent readers, 2 KiB rows,
every constraint at its default); 4 GiB at Q = 6 and 8 GiB at Q = 16 are the same path.

**Label: `client-triggerable · DDL role; the coordinator the attacker connects to, repeatable on every node`**

Reach: **alone** on any multi-node cluster. Lowest privilege verified by the arm's shape: **DDL role** (its own RF 1
keyspace reproduces the topology of stage 2). A **data role** on an admin keyspace is probable where adjacent token ranges
have different replica sets (N > RF), but untested (see Reason).

## Condition and how common

None outside the client, given a cluster of ≥ 2 nodes (routine). On a single-node cluster every range merges into one
sub-range and there is no fan-out.

## Blast radius

- **Which node:** the coordinator the attacker connects to (the responses are held on its heap; first error on its
  ReadStage building a local response). The attacker picks the coordinator freely.
- **Repeatable across nodes:** yes, by connecting to each node in turn or at once; each needs only a few (3 at 2 GiB,
  16 at 8 GiB) concurrent queries. Replicas send ≈ 100 MB bursts per query and drop some (internode reserve), which slows
  other tenants' reads on them too.
- **Other tenants:** while the coordinator lives, its ReadStage and heap are saturated (timeouts); after the OOM, clients
  using it as coordinator fail over, and the node's replica ranges lose one replica (Unavailable at RF 1 / CL ALL, degraded
  at QUORUM).

## Operator visibility

Slow `ALLOW FILTERING` range reads in `debug.log` via `slow_query_log_timeout` (500 ms); internode drops on replicas
("overloaded; dropping", dropped-message metrics); GC warnings. The fan-out itself is visible only in traces ("Didn't get
any response rows; new concurrent requests: N"). The operator could disable `allow_filtering_enabled` or enable read-size
thresholds (not tested as a defence here; `max_concurrent_range_requests=1` removed the OOM). Attribution by role via
`system_views.clients`; the audit log ships off.

## 3-b confirm

**Worthwhile**: the 3-instance stage-2 setup with authentication on; the attacker role gets `CREATE` on ALL KEYSPACES only,
creates its RF 1 keyspace, loads its rows and runs T at Q = 3; a victim role reads/writes another (admin, RF 3) keyspace
through the same coordinator and records success and latency. A second, more informative variant is the **data-role** one:
admin keyspace at RF 3 on ≥ 6 instances, attacker with `SELECT, MODIFY` on one table, CL ONE and CL ALL (merging
behaviour) — needs more instances than stage 2 used (owner's call).

## Reason

Every client step (a keyspace and table, rows placed by token, paged `ALLOW FILTERING` reads at a modest concurrency) is
available to a non-superuser; the guardrails that could interfere (`allow_filtering_enabled`, page size, read thresholds)
ship permissive for ordinary roles, and the fan-out is the server's own default behaviour after an empty batch. Stage 2
needed different owners for adjacent ranges, which a DDL role gets by creating an RF 1 keyspace on any multi-node cluster;
with an admin-made RF 3 keyspace the number of merged sub-ranges depends on N and the CL (`ReplicaPlans.java:774-792`), so
data role is likely on clusters with N > RF but not shown. No fault, restart or aid is needed. Hence **client-triggerable
alone at DDL role**, coordinator chosen by the attacker.

**Result.** The attack was confirmed **6/6** with authentication and authorization on: **3/3 at a DDL role** (the label's

### Deviations

1. Before creating roles, `system_auth` was raised to RF 3 with a full repair (admin, the yaml's own advice). With the
   shipped RF 1, the roles' rows could live on node 1 only and confound V2 after node 1 dies.
2. The LOAD lifetimes (attacker's data load, probes, traces) ran with every heap at 8 GiB, as stage 2's LOAD did. Between
   the load and the attack lifetimes the harness flushed, drained and restarted from templates (stage 2's clean start).
   These steps are harness mechanics, not attacker steps. Whether the attack works on memtable-resident data without a
   restart was not run.
3. The data-role variant ran on the same 3 instances (RF 2, N 3) instead of the "RF 3 on ≥ 6 instances" the analysis
   suggested. At RF 3 a client gets the same merge rule at CL ALL (blockFor 3), not QUORUM, which can merge adjacent sets
   that share 2 of 3 replicas. That case is code reading only.
4. The victim shares CPUs 30–31 with the attack client and the JMX sampler (light load).
5. In the Q = 1 phase, one `ReadTimeout` occurred in `T-auth-1` and `T-auth-3`, and in every DR lifetime (stage 2's T
   saw none). These are replica drops of the first dense sub-range: at QUORUM each remote replica sends ≈ 20 × 10.4 MB per
   query, and the DR lifetimes show 37–53 dropped large messages per replica. The OOM still came 1.1–1.7 s after Q = 3
   began. Whether the data-role OOM needs the 10 s drop waits was not separated: there was no drop-free data-role control,
   and this confirm checks reach, not the stage-2 verdict.
6. Node data ran on tmpfs `/dev/shm/3b-c10/` (stage 2's environment deviation 4), not under `/mydata/s4/3b-C10/`. It was
   removed at the end. One held SSH session on pc91 kept `systemd-logind` from emptying `/dev/shm` (stage 2's lesson).
