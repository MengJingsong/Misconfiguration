# R7 — stage 3-b client trigger check (2026-11-24)

**Case:** [`concurrent_writes+max_mutation_size--inflight-DOB-fill-R7`](../cases/concurrent_writes+max_mutation_size--inflight-DOB-fill-R7/concurrent_writes+max_mutation_size--inflight-DOB-fill-R7.md)
(true positive, `verified-OOM · deployment`, unprotected accumulation; validity-window caveat in its §4). Stage 3-a: not
run yet (the case file's own JIRA check: `known-open`, CASSANDRA-21656). Analysis only (no node run). Code references are to
the pinned 5.0.9 tree `systems/cassandra/source/`, paths below `src/java/org/apache/cassandra/` unless a file under `conf/`
is named.

**Mechanism.** On a replica, each `MutationStage` thread serializes an incoming mutation into its thread-local direct
`DataOutputBuffer` in `CommitLog.add`. A 16 MiB − 1 KiB mutation grows the buffer to ≈ 2S (3S during the copy), and it is
trimmed only at `close()`. Up to `concurrent_writes` = 32 threads hold such buffers at once, and no control bounds the sum.
At heap 2 GiB / direct 896 MiB the replica dies of `OutOfMemoryError: Direct buffer memory` (6/7 arms, 4/5 valid). At
1,024 MiB it died 2/5.

**Label: `client-triggerable given a small node (direct limit ≤ ≈ 1 GiB: the cassandra-env.sh value on a ≤ 4 GiB-RAM node, or an explicit MAX_DIRECT_MEMORY_SIZE) · data role (MODIFY); every replica of the written keys at once, repeatable on every replica set of such nodes`**

**Condition:** a small node, i.e. a direct-memory limit of ≈ 1 GiB or less. That is the automatic resolution on a ≤ 4 GiB-RAM
host, or an operator-set `MAX_DIRECT_MEMORY_SIZE`. How common: **occasional to rare** in production (small cloud VMs,
dev/test rings). The project's own guidance and most deployments use ≥ 8 GiB RAM. Secondary topology requirement for the
tested path: the target replicas receive the writes from ≥ 4–5 other nodes. That is routine in rings of ≥ 6 nodes.

## Blast radius

- **Which node:** the **replicas of the written keys**, not the coordinators the attacker talks to. With RF 3 every replica
  gets the same mutation stream, so the whole replica set of the chosen token range is pushed at once. The node with the
  smallest direct budget dies first. The attacker picks the range by partition key (16 vnodes per node by default,
  `cassandra.yaml:42`).
- **Repeatable:** yes, range by range. Each death needs ≈ 47–115 s of load. The 200 rewritten keys hold ≤ 3 GiB on disk.
  A replay of large mutations from the commit log on restart was not tested.
- **Other tenants:** writes and reads on the attacked range time out during the load (the client saw mostly
  `WriteTimeoutException`). After a death the range runs on fewer replicas, and losing two of three gives `Unavailable` at
  QUORUM. The coordinators carry heavy load too: on small coordinators their own native-frame direct memory is B9's risk
  (untested here).

## Operator visibility

Visible, but only as load. There are client `WriteTimeout`s, MutationStage active/pending at 32, dropped MUTATION messages,
"System.gc()" lines from `Bits.reserveMemory` in the replica's GC log, and the commit log and disk saturated. With hints on,
the coordinators' hint directories grow fast. No warning names the large values (column-value guardrail off). Audit/FQL
(off by default) would attribute the role and its 16 MiB INSERTs.

## 3-b confirm (not run): worth it?

**Not worth it as a privilege confirm.** The only client step is an INSERT with MODIFY, and the replica path is internode,
where the client's role plays no part. The open questions are about the deployment, not privilege:
1. a real RF 3 ring of small nodes with hints on, where the coordinators are small too;
2. the validity-window caveat of stage 2.

Each would need a multi-node ring of ≤ 4 GiB-RAM nodes (or explicit 2 GiB / 1 GiB pairs on ≥ 6 nodes) with the attacker
role and a victim role, at the cost of the stage-2 set-up (≈ 7 min per arm, 9 JVMs).

## Reason

The client part of the test arm is ordinary CQL: MODIFY on a table with a blob column, and 16 MiB − 1 KiB values sent at
CL ONE through several non-replica coordinators. No fault, operator action or diagnostic aid is in the verified arm, and the
coordinators' hints-off setting does not change the victim's load. What a client cannot choose is the victim's memory
budget. The holder's ceiling (≈ 1.2–1.7 GiB) exceeds only the direct limits of nodes with ≤ 4 GiB RAM or an explicit small
limit. Lowest privilege: **data role** (MODIFY). Reach: **given a small node**. Blast radius: every replica of the attacker's
keys at once, range by range.
