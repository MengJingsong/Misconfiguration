# B8 — stage 3-b client trigger check (2026-11-23)

**Case:** [`commitlog_max_outstanding_replay_bytes--accounting-mismatch`](../cases/bypass/commitlog_max_outstanding_replay_bytes-accounting-mismatch/commitlog_max_outstanding_replay_bytes--accounting-mismatch.md)
(true positive; deciding condition S `verified-OOM · diagnostic`, 1 GiB). Stage 3-a: [`B8.md`](../stage3-jira-check/B8.md)
(`known-fixed (incomplete)`, CASSANDRA-8639). Analysis only (no node run), by a fresh session that did not design or verify the case.

**Mechanism.** At start-up `CommitLogReplayer.handleMutation` bounds the replay queue by 1,024 mutations and 64 MiB of
**serialized** bytes (`db/commitlog/CommitLogReplayer.java:513-531`); a deserialized tiny-cell map mutation holds ≈ 15× its
serialized size, so a full queue holds ≈ 0.95 GiB. The queue only fills when the replayed volume exceeds one memtable pool
**and** the appliers stall; then a 1 GiB node OOMs during restart.

**Label: `client-triggerable given a restart that replays more commit log than one memtable pool (a commit-log restore) while flushes stall · data role; only the restarting replica of the attacker's keys, 1 GiB heap`**

**Condition and how common.** Two operator-side events at once: a restart that replays more than one memtable pool of
commit log (a commit-log archive restore, or sstables lost/removed while the log is kept) **and** an apply/flush stall
during that start-up. Each is occasional; together, at a 1 GiB heap, **rare**. The client's part is only to have written
dense tiny-cell collections that are still in the replayed log; blobs of the same size did not fail (0/3).

## Blast radius

- **Which node:** the replica(s) that own the attacker's partition keys, and only when they restart under the condition.
  The attacker can choose keys to place the dense mutations on chosen replicas, but cannot cause the restart.
- **Repeatable across nodes:** no, not by the client; each node fails only on its own restore + stall.
- **Other tenants:** the node fails to start (crash loop until the stall clears or the replay count property is lowered,
  case §4 item 4); its ranges run with one replica fewer; at RF 3 / QUORUM others see no error unless a second replica
  is also down. No runtime effect.

## Operator visibility

The restore and the stall are operator-visible events; the replay log lines (`Replaying …`, `Log replay complete`) and the
OOM during start-up point to commit-log replay. The dense map writes leave no warning (collection guardrails off); with
`items_per_collection_warn_threshold` set, the writes would be logged per user.

## 3-b confirm

Not worthwhile as a client confirm: the deciding steps (restore emulation, injected stall, restart) are operator-side and
would be re-run exactly as in stage 2. The only client step (a non-superuser role with MODIFY writing 5,900-entry maps)
is ordinary CQL and needs no confirmation.

## Reason

The heap the replay queue can hold is a function of what the attacker wrote (dense tiny-cell collections, 15× their
serialized size), and any data role with MODIFY on a table with a collection column can write that shape within every
default guardrail. But stage 2 showed that at defaults a plain restart never fills the queue (A: 10/10 started, even at
512 MiB); the OOM needed the replayed volume to exceed the memtable pool (sstables removed) and the appliers to stall
(Byteman), at a 1 GiB heap. Neither step is open to a client, and a client cannot cause the restart. The case is
therefore client-triggerable only given a rare operator-side condition, and it affects only the restarting replica.
