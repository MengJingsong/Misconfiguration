# B27 — stage 3-b client trigger check (2026-11-24)

**Case:** [`native_transport_receive_queue_capacity--v5-per-connection-allowance-outside-reserves`](../cases/native_transport_receive_queue_capacity--v5-per-connection-allowance-outside-reserves/native_transport_receive_queue_capacity--v5-per-connection-allowance-outside-reserves.md)
(true positive, `verified-OOM · deployment`, convergence by multiplicity with the connection limits at −1; heap 2 GiB 3/3,
also 4 GiB / 7,000 and 8 GiB / 14,000 connections 3/3; x86 `pc91`). Stage 3-a: `new` ([`../stage3-jira-check/B27.md`](../stage3-jira-check/B27.md);
owner alternative `known-open (partial: CASSANDRA-21091)`). Analysis only (no node run), by a fresh session that did not
design or verify the case. Code references are to the pinned 5.0.9 tree, paths below `src/java/org/apache/cassandra/`
unless they start with `conf/`.

**Mechanism.** Each v5 connection admits request bytes against its own exclusive `native_transport_receive_queue_capacity`
(1 MiB) and returns SUCCESS without touching the per-IP or global reserve (`net/AbstractMessageHandler.java:411-423`);
only the excess beyond 1 MiB is charged (`:425-436`). The connection count is unlimited (`config/Config.java:289-290`; the
`ConnectionLimitHandler` is not installed, `transport/PipelineConfigurator.java:239-245`). So *N* well-behaved connections,
each with ≤ 7 slow requests in flight, hold *N* × ≈ 0.57–0.88 MB of decoded requests on the NTR queue while every reserve
reads ≈ 0, no request is refused and no "bytes in flight" line is logged: 3,500 connections → `Java heap space` at 2 GiB.

**Label: `client-triggerable · data role; coordinator, repeatable on every node`** — SELECT on any table whose filtering
read (or other read) is slow enough to keep the 128 NTR threads busy (the verified arm: one 200,000-row partition,
5.7 MB), plus ≈ heap / 0.6 MB connections (3,500 at 2 GiB) from one or a few source addresses. MODIFY and DDL are not
needed. Same lowest privilege and request as B10 (whose confirm run held at SELECT only); B27 needs many connections
instead of a large window, and it is **silent** (reserves at 0, no "breached limit" line, no refusal).

**Lower privileges.**
- *Login role, no grants:* every SELECT on a non-readable table fails at once with `Unauthorized` on the NTR thread, so the
  threads never stay busy and no backlog forms (B10's NG-1: 1.0 M refusals, NTR pending ≤ 536, no Full GC). The tables a
  no-grants role may read (`service/ClientState.java:77-93`: `system.local`/`peers`, `system_schema.*`, virtual tables,
  `system_traces.sessions`/`events`) are small on a normal node. *Untested path (code reading):* any client may trace its
  own requests, and a traced QUERY writes its text into `system_traces.events` at parse time (`cql3/QueryProcessor.java:896`,
  before `authorize` at `:271`), with a 24 h TTL; a no-grants role could therefore grow `system_traces.events` and then run
  slow `ALLOW FILTERING` scans of it. Whether that keeps 128 NTR threads busy at a useful rate is not shown (cf. R24's and
  B26's notes on these tables); the label does not rest on it.
- *Pre-auth:* a v5 connection switches to v5 framing at STARTUP, before authentication
  (`transport/InitialConnectionHandler.java:114-124`); only *multi-frame* messages are refused before READY
  (`CQLMessageHandler.java:537-548`). One-frame QUERYs from a never-authenticated connection are therefore admitted on that
  connection's own 1 MiB allowance, decoded and queued, and refused only at execution (`transport/ServerConnection.java:69-72`
  via `Dispatcher.java:412`). Alone they build no backlog (B10's PA-1: 1.25 M fast refusals). **But as filler they are
  cheaper here than in B10:** no login, no bcrypt, no role on the connection, and still no reserve charged. Once something
  keeps the NTR threads busy — a few data-role connections with slow reads, or a node already loaded by legitimate slow
  requests — thousands of pre-auth connections can each park < 1 MiB of decoded requests uncharged (B10's MX-1 showed
  ≥ 86.6 % pre-auth bytes in the queue at the OOM via the refusal path; the within-allowance path has not been run). So:
  data role for reach **alone**; pre-auth is an amplifier given busy NTR threads.

## Condition and how common

None beyond the defaults (reach **alone**). The prerequisites are a readable table on which a filtering (or other) read
takes hundreds of milliseconds — most production tables qualify, and with MODIFY the attacker builds one in seconds — and
the ability to open ≈ heap / 0.6 MB connections to one node, which nothing at defaults limits.

## Blast radius

- **Which node:** the coordinator the attacker connects to (the decoded requests live there); the attacker picks it.
- **Repeatable:** yes, each node in turn or all at once (connections to each node; the port budget is per destination).
  Stage 2: 46–94 s from the first request at 2 GiB (plus the login time under auth). The node is killed by the shipped
  `-XX:OnOutOfMemoryError="kill -9 %p"` (`conf/cassandra-env.sh:209`) and stays down until an operator (or a supervisor)
  restarts it; nothing persists, so the attacker can repeat after each restart.
- **Other tenants:** every client of that coordinator waits behind ≈ 16 k queued requests (12 s "timed out before it could
  start") and then loses its connections at the kill; drivers fail over to other coordinators, so hitting every node takes
  the cluster's client service down. Even below the OOM the shape is a service loss: stage 2's 1,000-connection control
  timed out 44,785 requests before they started in 240 s. The scanned partition's replicas run ReadStage saturated.

## Operator visibility

Less than B10: **no** "Request breached limit(s) on bytes in flight" line, `Client.RequestsSize` ≈ 0, `RequestDiscarded`
+0, no `OverloadedException` to clients. Visible: `connectedNativeClients` and `system_views.clients` (thousands of
connections from one address, each with the authenticated role, so data-role connections are attributable and the role
can be dropped; pre-auth filler connections carry only an address), the auth pool's burst of logins, NTR `PendingTasks`
in the thousands, `TimedOutBeforeProcessing` rising, GCInspector pauses, filtering reads in the slow-query log. Audit
logging ships off. Only `native_transport_max_concurrent_connections[_per_ip]` (shipped −1) would stop it (stage 2's I2).

## 3-b confirm

**Worthwhile.** Two things differ from B10's confirmed run and are not covered by it: (1) 3,500 (and at larger heaps
7,000–14,000) **authenticated** logins through the 4-thread bcrypt pool, and whether the roles/permissions caches
reloading during the GC spiral (U7's lesson; it did not happen in B10's confirm) abort enough requests to turn the OOM
into service loss when the window per connection is only 7; (2) the within-allowance **pre-auth filler**. Run `T2` (2 GiB,
3,500 v5 connections × 7, 125 KB value) with `PasswordAuthenticator` + `CassandraAuthorizer`: admin creates and loads
`ks.t`; attacker role with **SELECT only** on `ks.t`, connections logged in before the load starts; a victim role on its
own table measuring success rate and latency. Claim arms (n = 1 each): `nogrant` login role × 3,500 (expected: no
backlog); data role on ≈ 20 connections + 3,480 pre-auth connections × 7 (expected: OOM with mostly pre-auth bytes, K ≈ 0);
optional, the `system_traces.events` no-grants path above. B10's 3-b harness (`logs/B10/harness/`, `NativeFlood3b`
with `--auth login|none`) plus stage 2's `--connect-threads` fits as is.

## Reason

Every step of T2 is ordinary client behaviour: many v5 connections, each with a small, well-behaved window inside its own
contract, sending a SELECT with `ALLOW FILTERING` on data the role may read. The admission that lets the bytes pile up is
transport code on the event loop, before authorization, and the only control that would bound it — the connection count —
ships disabled. The only privilege needed is the one that makes the requests slow: SELECT on a table with enough rows (or
MODIFY to write them). No operator-side condition is involved, the attacker chooses the coordinator that dies, and the
attack leaves no overload warning behind.

## 3-b confirm (pc99, 2026-11-24)

Fresh session, owner-approved confirm run (2026-11-24), 20:38–21:07 MST on `pc99` (node lock held for that span, then
released). **Result: confirmed.** With `PasswordAuthenticator` + `CassandraAuthorizer` on, a role holding **only SELECT on
`ks.t`** opened the verified arm T2 (3,500 v5 connections, all logged in first, then 7 in flight each, 125,000-B bound
value, `ALLOW FILTERING`, CL ONE, no `THROW_ON_OVERLOAD`). The node died with `java.lang.OutOfMemoryError: Java heap space`,
heap dump, shipped `kill -9` (1/1 attack run, A-1; node gone at *t* = 63.2 s, the OOME ≈ 33.9 s). The dump has the
stage-2 holder: 15,962 × `byte[125000]` = 1.996 GB, **93.6 %** of the heap, one per queued `QueryMessage` /
`Dispatcher$RequestProcessor` (15,926 / 15,925 = **4.55 per connection**, inside each 1 MiB allowance). The global reserve
read ≤ 117 B; no "breached limit" line, no refusal. The victim role, on its own keyspace and table, got **0.3 %** of its
requests through during the attack and **0 %** after it. The 3,500 bcrypt logins took 72 s on the 4-thread auth pool and
did not stop or slow the attack. Claim runs (n = 1 each): a login role with no grants (NG-1) and pre-auth connections
alone (PA-1) build no backlog. **Pre-auth filler on the within-allowance path** (not run in B10): **20** data-role
connections (140 slow reads) plus **3,480 never-authenticated** connections × 7 drove the node to heap OOM (MX-1). At
least **15,612 of the 15,752** queued requests (**99.1 %**) were pre-auth, and the reserves were still uncharged.

### Deviations

- Node `pc99` instead of stage 2's `pc91` (same experiment, hardware and image); the numbers are comparable. The node
  died at *t* = 63.2 s in A-1, inside stage 2's 46–94 s for T2.
- **Connect/login before the load (harness addition).** Stage 2's clients each started sending as soon as their own
  connections were up; here every attacker JVM connected (and logged in) all its connections first, and the load started
  on one go-file. This follows the analysis ("connections logged in before the load starts"). It also keeps the
  logins off the busy node, and the 10-s `STARTUP` time-out of later connections off a full NTR queue. The 24,500 first
  requests are sent at once, as in stage 2.
- One attack run (A-1), as directed. It reached the endpoint, so no fluke repeats were needed. The claim runs MX-1, NG-1
  and PA-1 are n = 1 each. There is no matched control or intervention under auth (stage 2 has them).
- The data set was built under auth by the superuser (stage 2's shape). The warm-up connection is the `attacker` role in

- In the OOM runs the clients were killed with `SIGKILL` 30 s after the node died, so they print no `TOTAL`. Their error

- Not run: the `system_traces.events` no-grants path (analysis: optional, the label does not rest on it), more nodes or

### Which code-reading claims held

- **"SELECT is the only privilege needed; MODIFY and DDL are not" — held.** The attacker held exactly SELECT on `ks.t`
  (its MODIFY, foreign-table SELECT and CREATE attempts were refused), and A-1 reached the OOM. The `allow_filtering`
  guardrail now applies, because the role is not a superuser. It did not block or warn: it is permissive by default.
- **"Each connection's requests are admitted on its own 1 MiB allowance, so no reserve is charged, nothing is refused,
  and no overload line is logged (silent)" — held under auth.** In A-1 K stayed ≤ 117 B while 1.996 GB of decoded
  requests (9.3 × heap/10) were queued, 4.55 per connection. There was no "breached limit" line, `RequestDiscarded` stayed
  0, and the clients saw no overload error. This is unlike B10's confirm, which logged the "breached" line in every run.
- **"3,500 logins ≈ a minute or two of auth-pool time; connections can be logged in ahead of the burst; other
  tenants' logins queue behind them" — held, with one part inferred.** 3,500 bcrypt logins took 72.2 s (A-1) and
  66.4 s (NG-1), about 50 per second on the 4-thread `Native-Transport-Auth-Requests` pool, with up to 444 pending. No
  login failed. Each attacker login waited 8.1–8.9 s, so a new tenant's login during that minute would wait about as long
  (inferred from the attacker's own logins; the victim did not log in then). An already-connected tenant was not
  affected: the victim stayed at 100 % with p99 3 ms through the login phase. Linear extrapolation gives about 4.5 min for
  stage 2's 14,000 connections at 8 GiB; that was not run.
- **U7's lesson (roles and permissions caches reloading during the GC spiral could abort requests and turn the OOM into
  service loss when the window is only 7) — did not happen.** The roles, permissions and credentials caches have a
  2,000-ms validity, so they must reload while the node thrashes. Even so, A-1 had no auth or cache ERROR, `AuthFailure`
  0, and client `err` 0. It reached the OOM about 34 s after the load started.
- **"Login role, no grants: unauthorized SELECTs fail at once, no backlog" — held** (NG-1: 8.47 M refusals, NTR
  pending ≤ 1,406, live QM ≤ 486, post-GC 117 MB). **Pre-auth alone: no backlog — held** (PA-1: 8.88 M
  `expecting SASL_RESPONSE` refusals, QM ≤ 1,080). Neither is free for other tenants: the victim fell to 70 % and 73 %,
  with about 125 ms median latency, from CPU load rather than memory. The refusal rate here (about 35–37 k/s from 3,500
  connections) is about 7 × B10's (about 4–5 k/s from 20 connections), and the victim's loss is similar.
- **"One-frame QUERYs from a never-authenticated v5 connection are admitted on that connection's allowance, decoded,
  queued, and refused only at execution" — held.** The PA-1 and MX-1 refusals are
  `ProtocolException: Unexpected message QUERY, expecting SASL_RESPONSE` responses to the 125 KB QUERYs. These come from
  `connection.validateNewMessage` on the NTR thread, after decode and queueing.
- **"Pre-auth as filler on the within-allowance path (not run in B10): once something keeps the NTR threads busy, thousands
  of pre-auth connections park < 1 MiB each, uncharged" — held, and with less than the analysis assumed.** Only 20
  logged-in data-role connections took part: 140 slow reads in flight, just above the 128 NTR threads. Together with
  3,480 pre-auth connections they drove the node to heap OOM (MX-1). The data role could have at most 140 requests
  outstanding, but the dump held 15,752 queued `QueryMessage`s, so **at least 15,612 (99.1 %) were pre-auth**. K stayed
  ≤ 160 B and no "breached" line was logged: the within-allowance path, not B10's refusal path. Time to OOM was longer
  (t_OOM ≈ 138 s vs 34 s): the queue oscillated (NTR pending 0–15 k, live QM 2.2–12.2 k) until it stayed full.
- **Blast radius "the coordinator the attacker connects to dies; every client of it loses service" — held.** The victim
  role has no relation to the attacker's role or table. It was unavailable from *t* ≈ 0 (A-1) and refused after the
  `kill -9` (0 % in the 30 s after). "Repeatable on every node": not run (single-node set-up).
- **Operator visibility — held as written.** There was no "bytes in flight" line, `RequestsSize` stayed at 0–160 B,
  `RequestDiscarded` stayed 0, and no `OverloadedException` reached any client. The data-role attack is visible as an
  `AuthSuccess` burst (+3,500 in 72 s) and a full auth pool, and is attributable by role. In MX-1 the 3,480 filler
  connections never authenticated (`AuthSuccess` +20 for the attack), so they carry only a source address; their
  refusals are not logged. NTR pending in the thousands was visible until JMX stalled in the GC thrash.
  `TimedOutBeforeProcessing` stayed 0 in the polls (they stalled). `system_views.clients` was not queried.

coordinator, repeatable on every node` (confirmed)**. With auth on, 3,500 logged-in SELECT-only connections × 7 drive the
