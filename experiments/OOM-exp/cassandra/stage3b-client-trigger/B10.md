# B10 — stage 3-b client trigger check (2026-11-24)

**Case:** [`native_transport_receive_queue_capacity--refused-v5-request-uncharged-drift`](../cases/native_transport_receive_queue_capacity--refused-v5-request-uncharged-drift/native_transport_receive_queue_capacity--refused-v5-request-uncharged-drift.md)
(true positive, `verified-OOM · deployment`; heap 2 GiB 3/3, also 4 and 8 GiB 3/3; x86 `pc72`). Stage 3-a: pending (`—` in
`_INDEX.md`). Analysis only (no node run), by a fresh session that did not design or verify the case. Code references are
to the pinned 5.0.9 tree, paths below `src/java/org/apache/cassandra/`.

**Mechanism.** In the default non-throwing mode a v5 one-frame request that fails `acquireCapacity` is still decoded and
queued on `Native-Transport-Requests` without its bytes being charged, and its release subtracts what was never added
(`transport/CQLMessageHandler.java:222-224`, `:245-260`, `:506-510`). With slow requests occupying the 128 NTR threads, the
decoded backlog grows to connections × client window: 15,220–15,404 decoded 125 KB requests (25.5 × the contract) at 2 GiB
→ `Java heap space`, dump, `kill -9`.

**Label: `client-triggerable · data role; coordinator, repeatable on every node`** — SELECT on a table holding enough rows
for a slow `ALLOW FILTERING` scan (the verified arm: one 200,000-row partition, 5.7 MB), or SELECT + MODIFY to write such
a partition itself.

**Lower privileges.** *Login role, no grants:* unauthorized SELECTs fail at once on the NTR thread, so the threads never
stay busy and no backlog forms (no slow request is available without a grant; the always-readable `system.local`/`peers`/
`system_schema` tables, `service/ClientState.java:77-93`, are small). *Pre-auth:* v5 one-frame requests from an
unauthenticated connection are decoded and queued the same way (R13 confirm PF-1: 142 k pre-auth BATCHes decoded, then
refused on NTR threads), so once something else keeps the 128 NTR threads busy — the attacker's own data-role reads, or a
node already saturated by legitimate slow requests — the queued bytes could be pre-auth filler. That needs either the data
role anyway or an outside load condition; not tested.

## Condition and how common

None beyond the defaults (reach **alone**). The prerequisite is a readable table on which a filtering read takes hundreds of
milliseconds; most production tables qualify, and with MODIFY the attacker builds one in seconds.

## Blast radius

- **Which node:** the coordinator the attacker connects to (decoded requests live there); the attacker picks it.
- **Repeatable:** yes, every node in turn or at once (≈ 23–31 s per node at 2 GiB, 20 connections from one host).
- **Other tenants:** the node dies (`kill -9` on OOM) and needs an operator restart; every client of it loses its
  connections; while the backlog builds, all requests on that coordinator wait behind 15 k queued requests (time-outs
  after 12 s), and the replicas of the scanned data run their ReadStage saturated.

## Operator visibility

`system.log` logs "Request breached limit(s) on bytes in flight (Endpoint: …, Global: …) and triggered backpressure";
`ClientMetrics` `TimedOutBeforeProcessing` rises and NTR `PendingTasks` reaches ≈ connections × window; filtering reads may
appear in the slow-query log. The connections carry the authenticated role (`system_views.clients`), so the attack is
attributable and the role can be revoked; audit logging ships off.

## 3-b confirm

**Worthwhile.** Run `T2` (2 GiB, 20 connections × 1,024, 125 KB value) with `PasswordAuthenticator` +
`CassandraAuthorizer`: admin creates and loads `ks.t` (200 k-row partition), attacker role with **SELECT only** on
`ks.t`, NativeFlood with SASL PLAIN login added; a victim role on its own table measuring success rate and latency. Two
things to watch: (1) U7's lesson — with `CassandraAuthorizer` the roles/permissions caches reload during the GC spiral and
can abort requests, which would drain NTR threads faster and might turn the OOM into GC service loss; (2) record whether
the bypass branch (`warned`, "breached limit" lines) is reached as before. Optional arm: the same with a pre-auth filler
connection set, to test the pre-auth note above.

## Reason

Every step of T2 is ordinary client behaviour: v5 connections with the drivers' default window, a SELECT with
`ALLOW FILTERING` on data the role may read, and the default non-throwing overload mode. The bypass is in the transport
layer, before authorization, so the only privilege needed is the one that makes the requests slow: SELECT on a table with
enough rows (or MODIFY to write them). No operator-side condition is involved, and the coordinator chosen by the
attacker is the node that dies.

## 3-b confirm (ms0640, 2026-11-24)

Fresh session, owner-approved confirm run, 08:07–08:53 MST on `ms0640` (node lock held for that span, then released).
**Result: confirmed.** With `PasswordAuthenticator` + `CassandraAuthorizer` on, a role holding **only SELECT on `ks.t`**
sent the verified arm T2 (20 v5 connections × 1,024 in flight, 125,000-B bound value, `ALLOW FILTERING`, CL ONE, no
`THROW_ON_OVERLOAD`) and the node died with `java.lang.OutOfMemoryError: Java heap space`, heap dump, shipped `kill -9`
(1/1 attack run, A-1). The dump has the stage-2 holder (15,630 × `byte[125000]` = 1.954 GB, 90.9 % of the heap, one per
queued `QueryMessage`/`Dispatcher$RequestProcessor`). The victim role, on its own keyspace and table, got 3.5 % of its
requests through during the attack and 0 % after it (node down until an operator restarts it). Claim runs (n = 1 each):
a login role with no grants (NG-1) and a pre-auth client (PA-1) alone build no backlog. But a pre-auth client can supply
most of the queued bytes once something keeps the NTR threads busy: with 2 data-role connections plus 18 pre-auth ones,
the node OOMed (MX-1), and at least 13,195 of the 15,243 queued requests were pre-auth. With a light slow load of 256
in flight plus 19 pre-auth connections (MX-2), 15.3 k requests queued and the victim got 3.4 % through, but the node did
not OOM within 240 s.
Evidence: [`logs/B10/`](logs/B10/) (one folder per run; `harness/` with the 3-b harness and `run-list.txt`). Small logs
stay on the node under `ms0640:/mydata/s4/3b-B10/logs/<run>/` (gc logs gzipped). The dumps (sha256 prefixes: A-1
`872a3345`, MX-1 `ed1a9150`), the data set, the copied tree and the debug logs were deleted.

### Deviations

- Node `ms0640` (8-core aarch64) instead of stage 2's `pc72` (40-core x86_64). The numbers are not pooled. Time to OOM
  was ≈ 155 s here against 23–31 s on `pc72`: the heap stayed full from *t* ≈ 16–33 s, and the node ran 127 Full GCs on
  8 GC threads before an allocation finally failed. The holder and its size (15.2–15.6 k decoded requests,
  U/*K_N* 25.5–25.9) match stage 2's 15,220–15,404 (25.5–25.7). Auth on and the platform change cannot be separated as
  causes of the longer time; this does not change the endpoint.
- One attack run (A-1), as directed. It reached the endpoint, so no fluke repeats were needed. The claim runs MX-1,
  PA-1 and NG-1 and the exploratory MX-2 are n = 1 each. There is no matched control or intervention under auth (stage 2
  has them).
- The data set was built under auth by the superuser (same shape as stage 2). The per-group window (`:<window>`) was
  added to the flood spec for MX-2 only.
- After the node died, the clients were killed with `SIGKILL`, so A-1 and MX-1 did not print their error texts. The
  attacker's `err` there is most likely read time-outs (SH: `ReadTimeoutException`), and the pre-auth `err` is the
  SASL refusal shown in PA-1. This was not captured per request.
- The victim's workload is light (10 ops/s, one connection, synchronous). `missed` ticks count a request still waiting.

### Which code-reading claims held

- **"SELECT is the only privilege needed; MODIFY and DDL are not" — held.** The attacker held exactly SELECT on `ks.t`
  (its MODIFY, foreign-table SELECT and CREATE attempts were refused), and A-1 reached the OOM. The filtering read is
  authorized by `SelectStatement.authorize` (SELECT only). The `allow_filtering` guardrail, which now applies because
  the role is not a superuser, did not block or warn: it is permissive by default.
- **"The admission bypass is transport code, before and independent of authorization" — held.** In A-1, K never went
  above E + 1 MiB (53.68 ≤ 54.74 MB) and `RequestDiscarded` stayed 0, while 15,472 decoded requests (25.9 × *K_N*)
  queued on NTR. The "breached limit … triggered backpressure" line was logged (the refused-but-dispatched branch,
  `Overload.BYTES_IN_FLIGHT`).
- **"The permission check runs at execution on the NTR thread, after decode and queueing, so queued bytes are held
  whatever their content" — held.** NG-1's `Unauthorized` and PA-1's `expecting SASL_RESPONSE` responses come from
  `Dispatcher.processRequest` on NTR (`connection.validateNewMessage`, `transport/Dispatcher.java:412`). In MX-1 the
  data-role group could have at most 2 × 1,024 = 2,048 requests in flight, but the dump held 15,243 queued
  `QueryMessage`s. So **at least 13,195 (86.6 %) were pre-auth requests**, decoded from never-authenticated
  connections and held uncharged until the heap ran out.
- **"Login role, no grants: unauthorized SELECTs fail at once, no backlog" — held** (NG-1: about 1.0 M refusals,
  NTR pending ≤ 536, QM ≤ 62, no Full GC). **The same holds for pre-auth alone** (PA-1: 1.25 M refusals, NTR pending
  ≤ 597, QM ≤ 76). Neither is free for other tenants: the victim fell to 71 % and 89 % with 75–102 ms median latency,
  from CPU load, not memory.
- **"Pre-auth filler could be the queued bytes once something else keeps the 128 NTR threads busy" (analysis: not
  tested) — held.** With the attacker's own 2,048 slow reads (MX-1), the node reached heap OOM with ≥ 86.6 % pre-auth
  bytes. With a light slow load of 256 in flight, the size a busy node might carry from legitimate clients (MX-2), the
  pre-auth connections queued 15.3 k decoded requests (1.92 GB). That meant service loss (victim 3.4 %, 47 % GC STW),
  but no OOM in 240 s (n = 1). The label stays at the data role for reach **alone**. A pre-auth client is an amplifier
  on a node whose NTR threads are already busy: occasional on a loaded production node, and no credentials are needed.
- **U7's lesson (roles and permissions caches reloading during the GC spiral could abort requests and turn the OOM into
  GC service loss) — did not happen.** A-1 and MX-1 have no auth or cache ERROR and `AuthFailure` 0, and both reached
  the OOM after about 2 min of Full GCs. (AUTH_RESPONSE messages run on the separate `Native-Transport-Auth-Requests`
  pool, seen in the StatusLogger output.)
- **Blast radius "the coordinator the attacker connects to dies; every client of it loses its connections" — held.**
  The victim role has no relation to the attacker's role or table. It was unavailable from *t* ≈ 0 and refused after the
  `kill -9`. "Repeatable on every node": not run (single-node set-up). **Time per node: not held on this hardware**
  (≈ 155 s here vs 23–31 s on `pc72`).
- **Operator visibility — partly.** The data-role attack is attributable: `AuthSuccess` +20 for the attacker's
  connections, with the role in `system_views.clients`. Pre-auth filler connections carry no role, only a source
  address. Their refusals are not logged; only the rate-limited "breached limit" INFO line and the client metrics show.
  `TimedOutBeforeProcessing` rose in A-1 (106 k) and MX-1 (16.6 k) but stayed 0 in MX-2, though 15 k requests were
  queued there.

**Label (updated): `client-triggerable · data role (SELECT on any table with a slow filtering read; MODIFY not needed);
coordinator, repeatable on every node` (confirmed)**. On a node already busy with slow requests, pre-auth connections
(no credentials) can supply ≥ 86 % of the queued bytes (MX-1: OOM; MX-2: service loss). A login role with no grants,
or pre-auth alone, builds no backlog. Default exposure under the shipped `AllowAllAuthenticator`: anyone who reaches the
native port.
