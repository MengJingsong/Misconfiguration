**Mechanism.** BIG-format index summaries are JNA-malloc'd outside `-Xmx` and `MaxDirectMemorySize`, and
`index_summary_capacity` is enforced only by the 60-min redistribution. A default STCS merge holds its inputs'
summaries, the output bloom filter and compression metadata, and the growing output summary (with a copy at each growth
and at the trim) at once → memcg kill in a 2,560 MiB container (A: on top of parked full-sampling summaries; B: one large
merge alone).

**Condition assessed:** **B** (`verified-OOM · deployment`, the more client-like one: no parked load): one default STCS
merge of four imported 7 M-partition sstables (1,000-byte keys) at heap 1,280 / direct 640 / container 2,560 MiB, killed
3/3. A (parked P imported, then merge of Q4) has the same delivery step.

**Label: `not client-triggerable (sstables delivered by nodetool import)`**

Reach: not client-triggerable as verified. A CQL-only analogue for B (a DDL role writing ≈ 28 M partitions with 1-KB keys
until STCS merges four ≈ 7 M-partition sstables) is plausible but untested; see below.

## Condition and how common

Not applicable as run. For the CQL analogue the condition would be a small node (≈ 2.5 GiB container) holding a table of
tens of millions of long-keyed partitions (≈ 30 GB of data on a 2.5 GiB node): **rare**.

## Blast radius

As run: the node that imports and compacts (operator-chosen). For the untested CQL analogue: every replica of the
attacker's table (RF chosen by a DDL role), each killed when its own large STCS merge runs — timing set by compaction, not
by the attacker, and repeatable only as often as such merges occur.

## Operator visibility

Index summary pool above its 64 MB target between redistributions (`IndexSummaryManager` MBean); a large compaction in
`nodetool compactionstats`; the kernel memcg kill names `CompactionExecutor` (`dmesg`), no `OutOfMemoryError` in the
Cassandra log. For a CQL analogue, the table's size and partition count would be visible long before (`tablestats`).

## 3-b confirm

Not worthwhile as a confirm (no client-reachable verified arm). If the owner wants the CQL analogue judged, it is a new
stage-1 design (deliver Q7 by CQL writes as a DDL role, natural flushes and STCS tiers to a 4 × 7 M merge), not a 3-b
confirm run.

## Reason

The schema and the row content of the verified arm are client-writable, but the decisive data shape — four equal
7 M-partition sstables with full-sampling summaries arriving at once — was produced offline and delivered with `nodetool
import`, a JMX-only operation (`ColumnFamilyStoreMBean.importNewSSTables`); the arms also drop the page cache as root.
These are steps no client can take, so the label is `not client-triggerable (sstables delivered by nodetool import)`.
Condition A additionally parks summaries by an import and uses JMX in its intervention, so it gets the same label. A
client might reach B's merge through ordinary writes and natural compaction, but that changes the timing and the summary
history of the inputs and was not tested.
