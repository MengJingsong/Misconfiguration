**Mechanism.** With one replica down, every CL=ONE write through a coordinator that is not a replica is acked by the live
replica and leaves a `HintRunnable` holding the whole mutation on the coordinator's unbounded MutationStage queue, while
the single hint writer is parked at the 3 × 32 MiB slab cap. `checkHintOverload` (5,120 tasks) never refuses because
`hintsInProgress` is a bare `CacheLoader` (`service/StorageProxy.java:203`, `:2764-2768`): a fresh zero counter on every
call (`:1585-1597`). At 64,000-B writes faster than the hint disk drains, the heap fills with hint tasks.

**Condition assessed:** arm T (3 instances, n3 stopped, n1 non-replica coordinator, 2 GiB heap, 64,000-B CL=ONE writes
from 200 threads); the same at 4, 8 and 15,872 MiB; condition X (1 MiB values) noted.

**Label: `client-triggerable given a replica down (within max_hint_window) · data role; any coordinator the attacker picks, repeatable on every live node`**

Reach: **given a replica down** (occasional to routine). Lowest privilege: **data role** (MODIFY on any table in a keyspace
with RF ≥ 2 that has a `blob`/`text` column).

## Condition and how common

One replica down, for less than `max_hint_window` (3 h by default). **Occasional to routine**: every rolling restart
(upgrade, config change) takes each node down for minutes, and node failures happen; a client sees a down replica as
`Unavailable`/timeouts at CL ALL or via the driver's node-down events (`system.peers` and protocol events are available to
any role). The attacker needs sustained write bandwidth above the coordinator's hint-disk drain rate (≈ 130 MB/s here).

## Blast radius

- **Which node:** the coordinator the attacker connects to (non-replica for the chosen keys); free choice of node.
- **Repeatable across nodes:** yes, against every live node in parallel (each as coordinator for keys it does not own),
  while one node is already down — so a second node loss can push ranges below QUORUM, and with all coordinators dead the
  cluster is down.
- **Other tenants:** writes through the stalled coordinator queue behind the hint tasks and time out; after the OOM,
  clients fail over, the dying coordinator loses the hints it held (the down replica then needs repair), and the dead
  node's own ranges lose a replica.

## Operator visibility

Before the OOM: `TotalHintsInProgress` (Storage metric) climbing past `MaxHintsInProgress` with no refusals; MutationStage
pending; hint-directory growth on the coordinator; the down node in `nodetool status`. The enforcement message "Too many
in flight hints" never appears (the check cannot fire). Attribution: high write throughput from one role is visible in
`nodetool clientstats` / `system_views.clients`; the audit log ships off.

## 3-b confirm

**Worthwhile**: stage 2's 3-instance setup with authentication on; the attacker role holds `MODIFY` on one admin-made RF 2
table and computes keys off n1 itself (`num_tokens` 16, no `initial_token`, to show the placement is a client choice); the
operator stops n3 (the condition); a victim role writes small rows through n1 to another table at RF 2 and records its
success and latency. One victim node, defaults, 2 GiB, as stage 2.

## Reason

Every client step — choosing the coordinator and keys, CL ONE, 64 KB writes at a high rate — needs only `MODIFY`
(`ModificationStatement.java:249`) and passes every default limit; the coordinator's only claimed bound on hint tasks
(`checkHintOverload`, `StorageProxy.java:1585-1597`) can never refuse because of the `CacheLoader` defect (`:203`,
`:2764-2768`), and the single-token placement of stage 2 only made the non-replica path universal, which a client gets by
key choice. The step no client can take is the replica being down, so the reach is **given a replica down**, at **data
role**, on any coordinator the attacker chooses.
