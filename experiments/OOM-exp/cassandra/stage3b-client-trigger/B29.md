# B29 — stage 3-b client trigger check (2026-11-24)

**Case:** [`../cases/cached_rows_fail_threshold--rfp-static-fetch-whole-partition/cached_rows_fail_threshold--rfp-static-fetch-whole-partition.md`](../cases/cached_rows_fail_threshold--rfp-static-fetch-whole-partition/cached_rows_fail_threshold--rfp-static-fetch-whole-partition.md)

pc72, 2 instances RF 2, 2 GiB coordinator heap: heap OOM 3/3 at Q = 8; C-agree, C-Q2, I-names 0/3; OOM at 4 / 8 / 12 GiB,
15,872 MiB survives at Q = 64 with 35/96 queries cancelled at the 5 s deadline). Stage 3-a:
[`../stage3-jira-check/B29.md`](../stage3-jira-check/B29.md). Analysis only (no node run), by a fresh session that did not
design or verify the case. Code references are to the pinned 5.0.9 tree `systems/cassandra/source/`, paths below
`src/java/org/apache/cassandra/` unless they start with `conf/`.

**Mechanism.** A filtering read with a predicate on a **static column** at a CL above ONE goes through replica filtering
protection (RFP). If the coordinating replica is **stale** (it missed a newer static value, and a partition deletion, that
another replica holds), its static row does not match the predicate, so it returns nothing for the partition and is "silent";
`addToFetch` marks the static row unresolved (`service/reads/ReplicaFilteringProtection.java:500-511`, `:510`). The completion
fetch then reads that replica's **whole partition** with the query's clustering filter, `RowFilter.none()` and
`DataLimits.NONE` (`:604-632`, `:623`, `:629-630`), locally on the coordinator (`:170-172`), serialized into one heap
`DataOutputBuffer` held until the protected partition is merged (`:182`). The rows never pass `addRow` /
`incrementCachedRows` (`:477-492`, `:319`), so `cached_rows_fail_threshold` charges 2 per query; the only bounds are the
32 READ permits and the 5 s read deadline. 8 concurrent queries on 262 MB stale partitions kill a 2 GiB coordinator in the
first wave.

**Label: `client-triggerable given a replica that missed the attacker's writes · data role; the stale replica (as
coordinator), repeatable after its restart until hints or repair catch up`**

Reach: **given an outside condition** — a replica that was down while the attacker wrote, and that is either back up with
its hints not yet replayed, or was down longer than `max_hint_window` (3 h) and has not been repaired since. Node outages
(restarts, upgrades, crashes) are routine; the stale window after a short outage is normally seconds to minutes, but the
attacker can lengthen it with its own writes (code reading, below); an outage past 3 h without repair is occasional. Lowest
privilege: **data role** — SELECT + MODIFY on a table that has a static column, in a keyspace with RF ≥ 2 (the attacker
writes every byte of the stale data itself); **DDL role** where no such table is granted. Under the shipped
`AllowAllAuthenticator` (`conf/cassandra.yaml:198`) anyone who reaches the native port has both; the condition still decides.

## Condition and how common

The client cannot make a replica miss its writes by itself at defaults: every write goes to every live replica, and a missed
or dropped write is hinted (`StorageProxy.java:2442-2463`). So the attack needs a replica outage during which the attacker
writes, and the attack must run while that replica is still stale:

- **outage longer than `max_hint_window` (3 h), no repair since** — the verified arm's condition (F1). Occasional (a node
  down for hours; 5.0 ships no automatic repair). The stale window lasts until the next repair, i.e. days;
- **any outage, attack before hint replay finishes** — routine (rolling restarts, upgrades, crashes); the window is short
  unless the attacker inflates the hint backlog ahead of its own DELETE + UPDATE (code reading, unverified).

The attacker is told about both edges of the outage by `STATUS_CHANGE` events, and must have prepared the wide partitions
beforehand (≈ 2 GB per replica for a 2 GiB node).

## Blast radius

- **Which node:** the stale replica, which the attacker makes the coordinator by connecting to it. Other nodes are not hit
  (a non-stale coordinator would fetch from the stale replica over the internode path, capped at 128 MiB per message, the B22
  path).
- **Repeatable:** on that node, yes: no query completed before the OOM (0/96 in T1–T3), so no read repair was written and
  the partitions stay diverged; after the restart (shipped `kill -9` on OOM) the same queries kill it again until hints
  replay or a repair runs. Across nodes: once per node that has an outage while the attacker writes — during a rolling
  restart, every node in turn. Killing a returned node while the next one is down for its own restart takes two replicas of
  a set down at once.
- **Other tenants:** every client of the killed coordinator loses its connections; at RF 3 QUORUM requests survive one dead
  replica, at RF 2 QUORUM (= ALL) they fail for its ranges; restart time includes commit-log replay; a node killed repeatedly
  also delays its own hint intake and repair.

## Operator visibility

Little before the kill: RFP logs nothing (K = 2 against a 2,000-row warn threshold), no byte read threshold applies to the
fetch command even when enabled (O-thr arm), no partition-size guardrail warns at default, and no client warning or
`OverloadedException` is returned. Visible: the preceding outage itself, pending hints (`nodetool listpendinghints`), large
partitions in `nodetool tablestats` (max partition ≈ 262 MB), slow queries in `debug.log` (`slow_query_log_timeout` 500 ms,
`conf/cassandra.yaml:1398`), and the heap dump (the shipped `HeapDumpOnOutOfMemoryError`), whose read commands name the table
and keys, so the owning role is attributable through the table's grants and `system_views.clients`. Audit logging ships off.

## 3-b confirm

**Worthwhile (moderate cost, as B23).** The privilege steps are plain CQL, but the condition is what decides, and the
verified arm modelled it with `nodetool truncatehints`. A confirm would run, with `PasswordAuthenticator` +
`CassandraAuthorizer`: admin creates the keyspace (RF 2) and a table with a static column and grants SELECT + MODIFY to an
attacker role; the attacker loads ≈ 16 wide partitions (≈ 4 GB per replica) with both nodes up; the operator stops node 1;
the attacker issues the DELETE + static UPDATE through node 2 (arm A: hints truncated by the operator, as F1 — the > 3 h
case; arm B: hints kept, the attacker first writing ≈ 1 GiB of filler through node 2, then attacking node 1 as soon as it is
up, to measure the hint-replay window); a victim role on its own table on node 1 measures success rate and latency. Optional:
8 concurrent queries on one diverged partition. On pc72 the data load is slow (31 MB/s disk, case §3.4 D2); a faster node
of the same class is better.

## Reason

Every client-side step of the verified arm is ordinary CQL for a role holding SELECT and MODIFY on a table with a static
column: write wide partitions, then a partition DELETE and a static UPDATE, then run a few concurrent single-partition
filtering reads on the static column at QUORUM (or ALL), connected to the replica of the attacker's choice. RFP's completion
fetch for the unresolved static row reads the stale replica's whole partition uncounted and unbounded, and nothing in the
privilege model gates it. What the client cannot do is make a replica miss its writes: that needs a replica outage, and the
verified arm also needed the hints for it to be gone (`truncatehints`, standing for an outage past the 3 h window with no
repair) or the attack to land before hint replay. Reach: **given a replica that missed the attacker's writes**
(outage: routine; past the hint window without repair: occasional). Lowest privilege: **data role** (DDL role where no table
with a static column is granted). Blast radius: the stale replica as coordinator, repeatable after each restart until it is
repaired, and node by node across outages.

**Result.** The attack was confirmed **4/4** with `PasswordAuthenticator` + `CassandraAuthorizer` on, by a **data role**

stayed at 100 %. Its QUORUM session through node 2 (RF 2) dropped to 0.0–1.2 %.

### Deviations

1. **`system_auth` raised to RF 2 with a full repair** (admin, the yaml's advice), so the roles live on both nodes.
2. **Node 2's `auth_read_consistency_level` was set to `LOCAL_ONE` at run time** through its `AuthProperties` MXBean
   ([`harness/AuthJmx.java`](logs/B29/harness/AuthJmx.java), `divA/authjmx-n2.txt`, `A1/authjmx-n2.txt`). This was done after
   the attacker's first divergence login was refused (above), and again after node 2's restart. It is an auth availability
   setting, not a capacity constraint, standing for a `system_auth` replication that survives one node down. Node 1 kept the
   shipped `LOCAL_QUORUM`. Without the change, no client (attacker, victim or default superuser) could open a new session
   through node 2 while node 1 was down.
3. **The arm-A hints were removed by hand.** `truncatehints` could not delete the open hint file for the down node 1
   (above). A restart of node 2 to close it failed: node 2 is not a seed, and the only seed, node 1, was down ("Unable to
   gossip with any peers", `runs/LOAD-n2b/`). With both nodes down, node 2's hint file for node 1 (2,138 B, the 32 divergence
   mutations) was deleted from its hints directory. This stands for an outage past `max_hint_window`, in which
   `StorageProxy` writes no hint at all. Node 1 (seed, 2 GiB) then started first, then node 2 (4 GiB). So in A1 both nodes
   had restarted after the divergence. Node 1's `b29.t` memtable was empty after its restart in every A lifetime.
4. **Load lifetime and harness mechanics.**
   - Both nodes ran at 8 GiB in the load lifetime, then were flushed (stage 2's load did the same). This is harness mechanics,
     not an attacker step.
   - Node 1's 2 GiB budget took effect at its restart, which ends the outage.
   - Unlike stage 2, nothing was compacted: node 1 read 4 SSTables.
5. **No templates.** One continuous cluster: A1 → A2 → A3 on the same diverged data, then arm B on separately prepared
   partitions. So A2/A3 are not independent clean starts. They are the "repeat after restart" the label claims.
6. **Pre-check not informative.** The attacker's CL ONE `stat` read through node 1 was served from node 2 (trace: "reading
   data from /127.0.0.2"), so it returned `x` and does not show node 1's state. Node 1's staleness is shown by the 8 completion
   fetches in every dump.
7. **Victim differences from C10.**
   - It ran at 10 ops/s per session, not 40.
   - It shares CPUs and loopback with both instances and the attacker (environment, as stage 2).
   - It runs three sessions because RF 2 on 2 nodes makes QUORUM = ALL.
8. **Scope memory events not captured.** The cgroup scope was gone after each kill. The OOM is the JVM's
   `OutOfMemoryError: Java heap space` with heap dump and the shipped `kill -9` (`oom-excerpt.txt`), not a kernel kill.

### Updated label

**Label: `client-triggerable given a replica that missed the attacker's writes · data role; the stale replica (as coordinator),
repeatable after its restart until hints or repair catch up` (confirmed)**

Confirmed at 2 GiB with authentication and authorization on, data role (SELECT + MODIFY on an admin's RF 2 table with a static
column), **4/4**:
- **F1** (an outage past the hint window): 1/1, plus 2/2 repeats after restart, one of them with a single diverged partition.
- **Inside the hint window:** 1/1, attacking at the native-protocol `UP` event, 3.9 s before the first hint dispatch.

The confirm narrows the condition and the preparation:
- **Short outages count.** Any outage during which the attacker writes is enough, if it attacks within seconds of the `UP`
  event (or later, behind its own hint filler, not measured).
- **One partition is enough.** One diverged wide partition is enough (8 concurrent queries on it).
- **Auth availability.** The attacker must be able to log in during the outage. A `system_auth` that cannot reach its read CL
  with one node down (RF 2 at `LOCAL_QUORUM`, as here before deviation 2) blocks the divergence step for every new session.

Not run: other nodes, and the rolling-restart sequence.
