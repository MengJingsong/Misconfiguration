**Mechanism.** The internode send/receive queues charge on-wire bytes, but a 1000-tiny-cell map write (10.8 KiB on the
wire) holds ≈ 181–213 KiB on the heap, and the coordinator keeps each sent `Mutation` (`hintOnFailure`) uncharged until
the replica answers. A replica that cannot drain (storage stall) fills with ≈ 37 k queued `Mutation`s ≈ 8 GiB of heap.

**Condition assessed:** **A** (`verified-gap · service-loss · deployment`): replica 8 GiB, 30 s storage stall, default
timeouts and threads, 4 coordinators — the only verified condition at default timeouts. B and D (`verified-OOM ·
diagnostic`) are the OOM conditions; they are discussed at the end.

**Label: `client-triggerable given a ≈ 30 s storage stall on a replica · data role; the stalled replica (Full-GC service loss, heap OOM occasional), steerable by key, repeatable only where a stall occurs`**

Reach: given a condition (replica storage stall). Lowest privilege: **data role** (MODIFY on a table with a collection
column, or on any table with clustering columns via a single-partition batch).

## Condition and how common

A storage stall of tens of seconds on one replica: **occasional** in operation (failing disks, saturated or throttled
cloud volumes, a hung I/O path). The client cannot create it but can exploit one opportunistically: a stalled replica is
visible to a client as rising latency/timeouts on keys it owns (token ownership is readable from `system.local` /
`system.peers` by any logged-in role, `service/ClientState.java` `READABLE_SYSTEM_RESOURCES`), and the attacker then sends
tiny-cell writes for those keys.

## Blast radius

- **Which node:** the stalled replica; the attacker steers load to it by choosing partition keys in its ranges. The 4
  coordinators stayed ≤ 8.2 % of heap after GC (one arm), so coordinators are not hurt at default timeouts.
- **Outcome:** Full-GC service loss for ≈ 2 min (98.7–99.3 % live, 3/3), recovered after the stall; heap OOM occasional
  (6 GiB replica 1/3; 8 GiB under a 600 s hang 1/2).
- **Repeatable across nodes:** only where a stall occurs; not a free choice of the attacker.
- **Other tenants:** requests whose replica set includes the stalled node lose that replica — at RF 3 / QUORUM still
  served, at CL=ONE/ALL or RF 1–2 timeouts or `UnavailableException`; hints for it accumulate on coordinators (hints on).
  The tiny-cell load turns a stall the node would ride out (blob control 0 Full GCs, 0/3) into a service loss.

## Operator visibility

Before: nothing specific at defaults — no collection-size guardrail, no queue-heap metric (CASSANDRA-7402 is open);
`internode_inbound` `using_bytes` stays below its on-wire limit. The batch variant logs the batch-size warning (keyspace,
table, size; no user). During: GC pause warnings (`GCInspector`), dropped-mutation counters on V. Attribution: a heap
dump/class histogram shows `Mutation`s of one table with large collections; with authentication on, `system_views.clients`
and `nodetool clientstats` name the roles connected to each coordinator; the audit log ships off.

## 3-b confirm

Feasible but of limited value: the decisive step (the stall) is operator-side, so a confirm run would show only that a
non-superuser role with MODIFY reproduces A (expected, as no step needs more). Needs: the stage-2 topology (pc96 loopback,
dm-delay on V), `PasswordAuthenticator` + `CassandraAuthorizer`, an attacker role with MODIFY on the map table only, a
victim role on another table measuring its CL=QUORUM/ONE success rate during the stall. Low priority.

## Reason

Every client step of condition A — the map schema shape, the 1000-entry writes, CL=ONE, many in flight over several
coordinators — is available to a data role with MODIFY on a table that has a collection column (the authorization check
is MODIFY only, `ModificationStatement.java:249`; no guardrail on collection size or item count ships enabled), and a DDL
role can create such a table itself. The step no client can take is the 30 s storage stall on the replica, so the reach
is `given <condition>`. The result at default timeouts is a service loss on the stalled replica (heap OOM occasional),
not a deterministic OOM. **Other conditions:** B (2 GiB coordinator, Byteman 100 ms per mutation, 60 s write/native
timeouts) and D (coordinator, two dm-delay-stalled replicas, 60 s write/native timeouts) reach a heap OOM of the
coordinator the attacker connects to, but need operator-raised `write_request_timeout` / `native_transport_timeout`
(server settings, not client-settable) plus slow replicas, and B a Byteman rule; at the default 2 s deadline the
coordinator's retention is bounded by rate × 2 s × 213 KiB (run 4). As OOM they are therefore `client-triggerable given
raised write/native timeouts (≥ 60 s) and slow replicas · data role` at best, and B as run is not client-triggerable
(Byteman). E (512 MiB, default timeouts) is still a candidate.
