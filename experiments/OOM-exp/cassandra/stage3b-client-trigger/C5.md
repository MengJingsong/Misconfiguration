**Mechanism.** With `hints_compression` on, every open `CompressedHintsWriter` grows its own direct `compressionBuffer` to
the LZ4 bound of the largest hint it has written and keeps it (`hints/CompressedHintsWriter.java:48-56`). One writer stays
open per unreachable destination until its file reaches 128 MiB. Nothing counts these buffers, so destinations × ≈ 15 MiB
(for 15 MiB hints) is bounded only by `MaxDirectMemorySize` → direct OOM on the coordinator.

**Condition assessed:** T (`verified-OOM · deployment`, claim limited to deployments that enable `hints_compression`): a
coordinator in `dc1` (heap 2 GiB, direct 896 MiB; 1/1 also at 1,024 MiB, what `cassandra-env.sh` resolves for a 4 GiB
node), 64 `dc2` nodes down, keyspace `{dc1: 1, dc2: 3}`, 62 writes of 15 MiB blobs at `LOCAL_ONE` → direct OOM in
`HintsWriteExecutor` 3/3.

**Label: `client-triggerable given a remote-DC outage of ≈ 50+ nodes (hints_compression on) · data role; the coordinator the attacker connects to, repeatable on every live coordinator`**

Reach: **given an outside condition.** Many nodes must be unreachable at once, inside `max_hint_window` (3 h). The case's
deployment scope, `hints_compression` on, is an operator feature switch (`conf/cassandra.yaml:122-128`, commented out by
default). Lowest privilege: **data role**, i.e. MODIFY on any table with a blob or text column in a keyspace replicated to
the unreachable nodes. A DDL role can shrink the work to about one write: its own keyspace with the down DC at RF = the DC's
node count hints every one of them per write.

## Condition and how common

At a direct limit D, ≈ (D − 130 MiB) / 15 MiB destinations must be unreachable with open hint files: ≈ 52 at 896 MiB, ≈ 60
at 1,024 MiB (4 GiB node), ≈ 130 at 2 GiB (8 GiB node), more on larger nodes. Smaller hints give smaller buffers, so
more destinations are needed. Such an outage means a whole remote DC of that size partitioned away (WAN loss, a DC
power event, a rolling-restart mishap), inside the 3 h window. On top of that the deployment needs `hints_compression` on
and small coordinator nodes. On clusters that large the combination is **rare**. The attacker cannot cause the outage. It
can only use one, and the outage is visible to clients as `DN` peers in `system.peers` / driver events.

## Blast radius

- **Which node:** the coordinator the attacker connects to. Hints are kept on the coordinator (`HintsWriteExecutor`, one
  writer per destination), so the attacker picks the node.
- **Repeatable:** yes, on every live coordinator in the surviving DC, at once (each holds its own writers). During the
  remote-DC outage the attacker can kill the whole live DC, so the cluster is fully down.
- **Other tenants:** everything on the killed coordinator fails (forced heap OOM, `kill -9`). The hints it held for the
  down DC are lost from memory; the files on disk stay. Every write in the outage depends on the live DC, so killing it
  stops all writes.

## Operator visibility

Before: many `DN` nodes in `nodetool status`; `TotalHints` and the hints directory growing fast; 15 MiB mutations in
`debug.log` only if they cross a warning threshold (none at defaults). After: `OutOfMemoryError: Direct buffer memory` on
`HintsWriteExecutor:1` at `CompressedHintsWriter.writeBuffer:55`. Nothing attributes the large writes to a role; the
audit log ships off.

## 3-b confirm

**Not worthwhile as a confirm:** the outside condition decides the case (the precedent of B6/B8/C1). The client steps are
plain INSERTs that need only MODIFY. If the owner wants it, the informative arm is the DDL variant on stage 2's ms0604 ring
(64 killed `dc2` instances), with authentication on. An attacker with `CREATE ON ALL KEYSPACES` creates
`{dc1: 1, dc2: 64}` and writes one or two 15 MiB rows. A victim role on another keyspace measures its success rate. This
would show the multiplicity in one write.

## Reason

Every client step (one table with a blob column, 15 MiB inserts at `LOCAL_ONE`, keys spread over the ring) is available to
a role with MODIFY, and no guardrail on value or mutation size interferes at defaults. The decisive inputs are outside any
client's control: ≈ 50–60+ simultaneously unreachable nodes at a 1 GiB direct limit (more on larger nodes), within the
hint window, on a deployment that enabled `hints_compression`. Hence **client-triggerable given a large remote-DC outage
(rare), data role**, with the coordinator chosen by the attacker and every live coordinator killable.
