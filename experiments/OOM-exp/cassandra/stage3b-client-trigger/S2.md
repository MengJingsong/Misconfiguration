# S2 — stage 3-b client trigger check (2026-11-23)

**Case file:** [`../cases/convergence/coordinator-local-write-backlog--combo/coordinator-local-write-backlog--combo.md`](../cases/convergence/coordinator-local-write-backlog--combo/coordinator-local-write-backlog--combo.md)
· **Stage 3-a:** [`../stage3-jira-check/S2.md`](../stage3-jira-check/S2.md) (`known-open (partial: CASSANDRA-19958, CASSANDRA-13213)`).
Analysis only (no node run). Code references are to the pinned 5.0.9 tree `systems/cassandra/source/`, paths below
`src/java/org/apache/cassandra/` unless a file under `conf/` is named.

**Mechanism.** A coordinator that is also a replica submits its own write as a `LocalMutationRunnable` to the unbounded
`MutationStage` queue (`service/StorageProxy.java:1674-1690`). At CL=ONE the client is answered when any other replica
acks, so the client slot and the native in-flight charge are released while the local write is still queued; an expired
local write is resubmitted as a hint on the same stage (`:2633-2652`). The backlog (≈ 61–63 k `Mutation`s) fills a 2 GiB
heap and persists after the client stops.

**Condition assessed:** **G1** (`verified-OOM · deployment`): RF=3 single DC, all three heaps 2 GiB, 11,000-B blob
writes at CL=ONE, 8 sessions × 256 in flight to the victim as coordinator, uncapped, no fault, 90 s. A (2-node, faster
8 GiB co-replica) and G2 give the same label.

**Label: `client-triggerable · data role; the coordinator the attacker connects to (2 GiB-heap nodes), repeatable on every node`**

Reach: **alone** (on nodes with a ≈ 2 GiB heap, i.e. the auto heap of a 4 GiB-RAM node; at 4 GiB heap G1 gave GC service
loss 3/6, no OOM). Lowest privilege: **data role** (MODIFY on any table).

## Condition and how common

None beyond the deployment size: the OOM was verified at a 2 GiB heap (what `cassandra-env.sh` gives a 4 GiB-RAM node);
4 GiB gave 0/6 OOM but GC service loss 3/6 (97–98 % live), and 8 GiB survived a 90 s burst. Larger nodes need a longer or
faster burst (untested).

## Blast radius

- **Which node:** the coordinator the attacker connects to (it must also be a replica of the written keys, which the
  attacker arranges). The other replicas stayed ≤ 25 % live.
- **Repeatable:** yes, on every node (connect to each and write its own keys); the backlog persists after the client stops
  (one arm OOMed 120 s after its load ended), so the attacker can move on before the node dies.
- **Other tenants:** the dead coordinator's clients lose their connections; one replica lost per replica set; repeating
  on two nodes of an RF 3 set makes QUORUM unavailable for those ranges. Before death, 97–99 % live heap means very long
  GC pauses and timeouts for everyone on that node.

## Operator visibility

`MutationStage` pending in `nodetool tpstats` grows to tens of thousands; dropped (self-dropped) `MUTATION_REQ` counters
and hint submissions on V; GC warnings. No guardrail or log warning names the client. Attribution: `system_views.clients`
/ `nodetool clientstats` show the connections and (with authentication) the role; write metrics per table identify the
table. Audit log ships off.

## 3-b confirm

**Worthwhile.** The whole arm is client traffic. Needs: a 3-node ring with 2 GiB heaps (stage-2 run 2 harness on
aarch64, or x86 nodes), `PasswordAuthenticator` + `CassandraAuthorizer`, an attacker role with MODIFY on its table only,
and a victim role on another table at CL=QUORUM through the same and another coordinator, measuring success and latency.
Optionally a 4 GiB arm with a longer burst to bound the budget dependence.

## Reason

Every step of G1 is a CQL client step available with MODIFY on a replicated table: plain blob INSERTs, CL=ONE, routing to
the node that replicates the key, and a high in-flight count. No guardrail restricts the CL at defaults, no rate or
connection limit ships on, and the only bounds on the request (client window, native in-flight bytes) are released when
another replica acks, while the coordinator's own copy stays on the unbounded `MutationStage` queue
(`StorageProxy.java:1676` `maybeExecuteImmediately`) and is re-queued as a hint when it expires (`:2633-2652`). No
operator-side condition is needed beyond a ≈ 2 GiB heap, which is a documented deployment size, so reach is `alone`.
