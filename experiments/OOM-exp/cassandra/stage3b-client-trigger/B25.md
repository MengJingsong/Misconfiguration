# B25 — stage 3-b client trigger check (2026-11-24)

**Case file:** [`../cases/profileload-capacity--read-sampler-pins-slab-regions/profileload-capacity--read-sampler-pins-slab-regions.md`](../cases/profileload-capacity--read-sampler-pins-slab-regions/profileload-capacity--read-sampler-pins-slab-regions.md)
· **Stage 3-a:** [`../stage3-jira-check/B25.md`](../stage3-jira-check/B25.md) (`new`).
Analysis only (no node run). Code references are to the pinned 5.0.9 tree `systems/cassandra/source/`, paths below
`src/java/org/apache/cassandra/` unless a file under `conf/` is named.

**Mechanism.** The read-path samplers of `nodetool profileload` / `toppartitions` (`READS` = `FrequencySampler.summary`,
`READ_ROW_COUNT`, `READ_TOMBSTONE_COUNT`, `READ_SSTABLE_COUNT` = `MaxSampler.queue`) keep the partition-key `ByteBuffer` by
reference. For a read served from a memtable under the shipped `heap_buffers` allocator, that key is a slice of a 1 MiB on-heap
slab region, so each retained entry keeps its whole region alive after the memtable is flushed and the pool has stopped
counting it. `finishSampling` leaves the entries in place until the next `beginSampling` on the same table. Profiling table
after table therefore stacks ≈ 245–280 MiB per table. Verified: heap OOM 3/3 at 2 GiB after 7–8 tables, and at every
ladder step up to 15,872 MiB (3/3 there, after 54–55 tables).

**Condition assessed:** T (build U, 2 GiB heap, all defaults): an operator runs `nodetool profileload b25 t<k>` with the
default options on one table after another, while a client writes ≈ 1.06 MiB partitions into the table being profiled and
reads each one back (CL ONE, 28 partitions/s).

**Label: `client-triggerable given an operator-run profileload/toppartitions of several of the attacker's tables, one after another (≈ 3.5 tables per GiB of heap; rare) · DDL role; the profiled node only`**

Reach: **given an outside condition.** The trigger, starting a sampler, is admin only. A sampler is disabled until
`beginSampling` (`metrics/Sampler.java:39`, `:106`, `:108-112`, `:121-124`). The only callers are the JMX operations
`ColumnFamilyStoreMBean.beginLocalSampling` (`db/ColumnFamilyStore.java:2056-2059`), `StorageServiceMBean.samplePartitions`
(`service/StorageService.java:6533-6546`) and `startSamplingPartitions` (`:6569-6593` → `metrics/SamplingManager.java:193-209`),
used by `nodetool profileload`/`toppartitions` (`tools/NodeProbe.java:567-577`, `tools/nodetool/TopPartitions.java:25`).
Nothing under `cql3/`, `transport/`, `auth/` or `db/virtual/` references a sampler (grep). So as run, the label would be
`not client-triggerable (nodetool profileload)`. The client's part is everything else: the traffic that the samplers record
(memtable-served reads of keys that sit in distinct slab regions) and the flushes that drop the regions out of the pool's
count. An operator runs profileload/toppartitions for its own reasons, to find hot partitions, so the attacker waits for it,
as C7's attacker waits for a repair. The attacker can make its own tables the hot ones, which is what those commands are run
to find. But it cannot start them or choose their time, and the verified multiplicity needs the operator to profile **many
distinct** attacker tables. Lowest privilege: **DDL role** (own tables, any number: `tables_fail_threshold` −1). A **data role**
suffices only with SELECT + MODIFY on at least ≈ 7 tables at 2 GiB (54 at 15,872 MiB), e.g. a keyspace-level grant on an
admin keyspace with that many tables. That is plausible at small heaps, rare at large ones.

## Condition and how common

An operator who runs `nodetool profileload` or `toppartitions` (`TopPartitions extends ProfileLoad`) on the node, on **one
attacker table after another**, while the attacker's read-after-write traffic runs on each. No session may repeat on a table
before the OOM, because a new `beginSampling` on a table releases that table's share (arm D: −276 regions). The OOM needs
≈ 7 such tables at 2 GiB, 15 at 4 GiB, 30 at 8 GiB and 54–55 at 15,872 MiB (≈ 3.5 tables per GiB). These are diagnostic
commands, not routine maintenance. Per-table runs over several tables happen when an operator looks for a hot spot, and
heavy traffic on the attacker's tables invites exactly that. Over 7 tables: **occasional to rare**. Over 54: **rare**. The
retained entries also outlive the session, so the profiled tables do not all have to be profiled in one sitting. They only
have to be profiled before the next profileload of the same table or a restart.

Untested variants (code reading):

- **One all-tables command** (`nodetool profileload` with no arguments samples every table at once:
  `tools/nodetool/ProfileLoad.java:84`, `metrics/SamplingManager.java:80-84`). One occasional operator action would then be
  enough. But the attacker would have to produce all the pinned regions inside one 10 s window: ≥ 7 tables × ≈ 300
  memtable-served keys in distinct regions ≈ 2 GiB written or resident in memtables within that window at 2 GiB. That needs
  ≈ 200 MiB/s of writes against a 512 MiB pool, and the 2 GiB OOM is plausible but untested. At 15,872 MiB it would need
  ≈ 13 GiB of distinct regions in 10 s: not reachable.
- **Scheduled jobs** (`-i`, `service/StorageService.java:6569-6593`) re-begin every table of the job each interval, so they
  keep at most one window's worth per table and do not stack beyond the all-tables case.

## Blast radius

- **Which node:** only the node whose samplers the operator starts (nodetool talks to one node's JMX). On that node, the
  pinned regions are those of keys it read locally during the window. In T (single node) that is every read. On a cluster,
  a DDL role can make every node a replica that reads every key: its own keyspace at RF = N, read at CL ALL so that each
  replica runs a local read (data or digest) through `executeLocally` (`db/ReadCommand.java:461`). Then whichever node is
  profiled pins (code reading). A data role on an admin keyspace (RF 3) reaches the replicas of the keys it chooses.
- **Repeatable:** not at will. Each OOM needs another operator profiling sequence on another node, and a restart clears the
  leftovers. An operator who profiles the same tables on every node in turn (e.g. `nodetool -h` over the ring) would take
  down each node it profiles.
- **Heap size:** OOM at every budget up to 15,872 MiB (3/3 there). Larger heaps only need more profiled tables.
- **Other tenants:** the session before the OOM is already GC-bound (Full GCs back to back). Then the node is killed
  (`OnOutOfMemoryError=kill -9`). Requests it coordinates fail, its ranges lose a replica until restart, and hints for it
  accumulate on the others.

## Operator visibility

Unusually visible, because the operator is running the trigger. Each profileload prints the top keys per table, which are
the attacker's keys and table. `system_views.clients` names the role per connection when authentication is on. The heap
grows ≈ 245 MiB per profiled table while `AllMemtablesOnHeapDataSize` stays low (154–185 MiB at the T OOMs): the growth
appears in GC logs and heap-used metrics but in no memtable or sampler metric. The OOM dump shows `byte[1048576]` reachable
only through `MaxSampler.queue` / `FrequencySampler.summary`. A profileload with no traffic on each profiled table releases
that table's share (arm D's D2), but nothing tells the operator to do it. The audit log ships off.

## 3-b confirm

**Not worthwhile as a confirm** (the operator step decides, as for C7 and B15). The client's steps are plain CQL INSERT and
SELECT on its own tables, whose permissions are not in doubt. Under authentication, the only open questions would be:
(a) whether a `CassandraAuthorizer` roles/permissions-cache reload timeout during the GC-bound last session (U7's effect,
`roles_validity`/`permissions_validity` 2000 ms, `conf/cassandra.yaml:300`, `:327`) delays the OOM by one table; it cannot
release regions already pinned, which are strongly reachable from the samplers; and (b) the RF = N / CL ALL cluster form. If
the owner wants the reach widened, the all-tables single-command variant (one occasional operator action, ≥ 7 attacker tables,
≈ 200 MiB/s for 10 s at 2 GiB) is a new stage-1 design, not a 3-b confirm run.

## Reason

The heap is exhausted by sampler entries recorded on the attacker's own reads, and the attacker supplies everything that
makes them expensive: keys spaced one per slab region, reads served from memtables, and the writes that flush those
memtables out of the pool's count. That needs SELECT and MODIFY on its tables (`SelectStatement.java:252`,
`ModificationStatement.java:249`), and its own tables (DDL role) to reach the table count. But the samplers record nothing
unless an operator enables them over JMX (`Sampler.java:108-112`, `ColumnFamilyStore.java:2056-2059`), and 5.0.9 has no CQL
path to them. The verified multiplicity needs the operator to profile ≈ 3.5 distinct attacker tables per GiB of heap, one
after another. So the OOM is client-triggerable only given that operator action (rare at the verified sequence; as run,
`not client-triggerable (nodetool profileload)`), at DDL role, and only on the node the operator profiles.
