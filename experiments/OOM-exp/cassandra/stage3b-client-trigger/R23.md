fresh session that did not design or verify the case. Related: [B8](B8.md) (same bound, restore emulation + Byteman flush

paths below `src/java/org/apache/cassandra/` unless they start with `conf/`.

**Mechanism.** A point-in-time restore with the shipped commit-log archiving copies every archived segment back
(`db/commitlog/CommitLogArchiver.java:270-330`, called from `db/commitlog/CommitLog.java:194`) and replays them all
(`CommitLog.java:200-207`); the restore point is checked only inside the apply task (`db/commitlog/CommitLogReplayer.java:296`,
`:502-510`). With the data volume slow, all 32 appliers park on the full memtable pool while `main` keeps deserializing;
the replay queue's bound counts **serialized** bytes and futures (`CommitLogReplayer.java:513-531`, 1,024 / 64 MiB), and a
dense tiny-cell map mutation holds ≈ 14× its serialized size, so at 1 GiB the queue holds ≈ 620–640 MiB at 700–731
mutations (under both limits) beside the 256 MiB pool → `Java heap space` during start-up.

Reach: **given** a point-in-time restore from the commit-log archive onto a slow data volume at a small heap (rare). Lowest privilege: **data role** (MODIFY on a table with a non-frozen collection column).

**Label: `client-triggerable given a point-in-time restore from the commit-log archive onto a node whose data volume is slow (≈ 100 ms write latency) at a heap ≤ 1,280 MiB · data role; only the restored replica(s) of the attacker's keys (every restored node in a cluster-wide restore)`**
— the operator-side events together are **rare**.

## Condition and how common

Three operator-side or environmental conditions at once: commit-log archiving enabled (off by default), a point-in-time
restore performed onto the node, and a slow data volume during that restart, at a small heap (≤ 1,280 MiB). Archiving and
small heaps are each occasional; a PITR is rare; a slow restore target is occasional (cloud volumes at their IOPS cap).
Together **rare**. The client's part is only to have written enough dense tiny-cell collection mutations (≈ 1,100, about
70 MiB serialized) between the restored snapshot and the restore point — or after it, with back-dated timestamps — on the
restored node. Mixed with other tenants' sparser mutations the queue's U/K falls, so the attacker's writes must dominate the
replayed window on that node (a quiet window or a high write rate).

## Blast radius

- **Which node:** the restored node(s) that hold the attacker's mutations in their archived log: the replicas of the
  attacker's partition keys, steerable by key. A client cannot start the restore.
- **Repeatable across nodes:** not by the client. But a PITR is usually cluster-wide (every node restored to the same
  point); if the restored nodes share the slow storage class and the small heap, every replica of the attacker's keys fails
  to start, and with dense writes spread over all token ranges, every restored node — the restored cluster does not come up.
- **Other tenants:** the restored node crash-loops in start-up replay (each start re-replays the same segments) until the
  operator lowers `cassandra.commitlog_max_outstanding_replay_count` (the intervention, count 64, started 3/3), raises the
  heap (≥ 1,536 MiB), or moves the data directory to faster storage; the restore's recovery time for every tenant is
  extended accordingly. No runtime effect outside the restore.

## Operator visibility

The restore, the archiving configuration and the volume's latency are operator-side and visible; the start-up log shows
`Replaying …` (with the restored segments) and the OOM before `Log replay complete`; thread dumps show 32/32
`MutationStage` threads in `MemtableAllocator$SubAllocator.allocate`. The dense map writes leave no warning (collection
guardrails off); with `items_per_collection_warn_threshold` set they would be logged per write. Attribution after the fact
is possible from the replayed table (the heap dump shows the deserialized `ArrayCell`s of the attacker's table).

## 3-b confirm

Not worthwhile as a client confirm: the deciding steps (archiving configuration, the point-in-time restore, the slow data
volume, the small heap) are operator-side or environmental and would be re-run exactly as in stage 2. The only client step
(a non-superuser role with MODIFY writing 5,900-entry maps over the native protocol) is ordinary CQL and needs no
confirmation (B8 and B15 make the same call).

## Reason

What the replay queue holds is a function of what the attacker wrote (dense tiny-cell collections, ≈ 14× their serialized
size), and any data role with MODIFY on a table with a non-frozen collection column can write that shape within every
default guardrail; client-chosen timestamps even let post-PIT writes into the applied set. But stage 2 showed that the OOM
needs a point-in-time restore from the commit-log archive (off by default, an operator procedure) **and** a slow data
volume during the restart (N1 on the plain disk started) at a heap ≤ 1,280 MiB (1,536 MiB started). None of these is open
to a client, so the case is client-triggerable only given a rare operator-side condition, and it affects only the restored
node(s) — all of them in a cluster-wide restore that meets the condition.
